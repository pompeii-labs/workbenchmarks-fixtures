I Estrazione · Pompei, Via Plinio 14

The town stopped in 79 AD. We stop for twenty-seven seconds.

Lapillo is a nine-stool espresso bar four minutes from the gates of the ruins. Eighteen grams in, thirty-six out, and nobody rushes the pour. Not even Vesuvius.

II Crema

Let it settle. The stripes are the bean talking.

That tiger mottling is gas and oil leaving a coffee roasted nine days ago, across the road, by us. When the crema goes quiet and hazel-gold, the shot is ready. We don't serve it before.

Successus the barista was here — and so was his tamper

III Vapore

Milk from Agerola, stretched to wet paint.

Whole milk from cows in the Lattari hills, half an hour up the road. A short hiss to pull air in, then a long roll until it's glossy, sixty degrees, and too small to see a bubble. Steam rises here. Not ash.

IV Disegno

A rosetta, poured close, pulled through.

Pompei has always painted on walls. We paint on crema. Tip in low, rock the jug, step back, pull a line through the middle. It takes four seconds and about four thousand cups to learn.

V Servito

At the counter, standing, as Naples intended.

Cappuccino, €1.80. Drink it at the marble while it's still trembling, or take a stool and stay a while. The ruins have waited two thousand years. They'll wait ten minutes more.

Why Lapillo

Small, local, and a little bit stubborn.

A lapillo is one of the little pumice stones that fell on Pompei for eighteen hours in 79 AD. We named the bar after the smallest thing in the story. That's the scale we like to work at.

  • I

    Roasted across the road

    Three-kilo batches on a drum roaster in our back room, twice a week. A house espresso of Brazilian and Ethiopian beans for the classic Neapolitan cup, plus one rotating single origin for people who want to argue about it.

  • II

    Neapolitan, not nostalgic

    Short, hot, and at the counter, with a glass of water first. Caffè sospeso is always on: pay for two, and the second is waiting on the board for whoever needs it.

  • III

    Before the gates open

    We open at 6:30, so guides, guards, and early visitors get a proper cup before the Scavi open at nine. Tell us which house you're seeing and we'll tell you where the shade is.

Il banco

A short menu, like a thermopolium.

Ancient Pompei had about 150 street counters serving hot food and drink. Ours is one of the few that takes cards. Prices are for the counter. Add €0.50 at a table.
